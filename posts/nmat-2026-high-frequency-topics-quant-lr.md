Success in the NMAT by GMAC exam requires a different strategy than CAT. While CAT tests your depth of understanding with complex, multi-concept questions, NMAT tests your breadth of knowledge and speed. 

Because NMAT has equal sectional weightage (36 questions per section), you must identify the **high-frequency topics** that appear consistently to allocate your study time effectively.

In this guide, we list the most frequently tested topics in NMAT Quantitative Skills and Logical Reasoning based on analysis of past papers.

---

## High-Frequency Topics: Quantitative Skills

The Quant section in NMAT is a mix of mathematics, Data Interpretation (DI), and Data Sufficiency (DS). 

| Topic | Average Questions (Out of 36) | Priority Level | Key Focus Areas |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Arithmetic** | 10–12 Questions | High | Percentages, Profit & Loss, Time & Work, TSD |
| **Modern Mathematics** | 6–8 Questions | High | Permutations & Combinations (P&C), Probability |
| **Data Interpretation** | 8 Questions (2 Sets) | High | Table charts, Bar/Line graphs (calculation speed) |
| **Data Sufficiency** | 4 Questions | Medium | Number properties, basic equations |
| **Algebra & Number Systems** | 4 Questions | Medium | Logarithms, progressions, divisibility rules |
| **Geometry** | 2 Questions | Low | Basic mensuration, coordinate geometry |

### Modern Math is Critical in NMAT
Unlike CAT, which rarely asks more than 2 questions on Permutations, Combinations, and Probability, NMAT places a huge emphasis on this area. You must be comfortable with arrangements, selections, coin tosses, and dice probability.

### Data Interpretation is Speed-Based
NMAT DI sets do not involve complex logical puzzles like CAT. Instead, they involve simple tables or charts with calculation-heavy questions. Learn to calculate percentages and ratios in your head.

## High-Frequency Topics: Logical Reasoning

NMAT's LR section is unique because it contains a balanced mix of **Verbal Reasoning** (critical thinking) and **Analytical Reasoning** (puzzles and series).

| Topic | Average Questions (Out of 36) | Priority Level | Key Focus Areas |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Verbal Reasoning** | 12–14 Questions | High | Statement-Assumption, Course of Action, Syllogisms |
| **Input-Output** | 4–5 Questions (1 Set) | High | Finding step-by-step alphanumeric patterns |
| **Arrangements & Puzzles** | 8 Questions (2 Sets) | High | Linear/Circular seating, scheduling |
| **Coding-Decoding & Series** | 4 Questions | Medium | Alphabet coding, number series patterns |
| **Blood Relations & Directions**| 4 Questions | Medium | Family trees, distance calculation |

### Verbal Reasoning is the Secret to Speed
Verbal Reasoning questions do not require calculations or tables. You read the short prompt and select the logical assumption or course of action. If you train your critical reasoning, you can answer these 12–14 questions in under 10 minutes, leaving extra time for complex puzzles.

### Input-Output Sets
These questions present a code transformation process. If you can decode the pattern of step 1 and step 2, you can answer all 5 questions instantly. Practice at least 20 different Input-Output models during preparation.

## Recommended Practice Sequence for NMAT 2026

- **Step 1:** Master Arithmetic concepts (percentages, ratios) to support both Quant and Data Interpretation.
- **Step 2:** Study Permutations, Combinations, and Probability. These are high-scoring and frequent in NMAT.
- **Step 3:** Practice Verbal Reasoning daily. Focus on identifying logical fallacies in assumptions.
- **Step 4:** Build speed in decoding Input-Output patterns and number series.
