Symbiosis International (Deemed) University allows candidates to take the Symbiosis National Aptitude Test (SNAP) up to **three times** in a single admission cycle. The university then considers your **best score out of the attempts** for shortlisting.

This 3-attempt structure has changed how students approach the exam. It reduces the anxiety of a "bad day," but it also demands a clear, strategic registration and preparation plan.

In this guide, we will analyze how the 3-attempt system works, discuss whether you should take all three attempts, and present a strategy to maximize your score across attempts.

---

## How the 3-Attempt System Works

- **Test Window:** SNAP is conducted on three separate dates in December (usually Slot 1 in early December, Slot 2 in mid-December, and Slot 3 in late December).
- **Registration Choice:** When registering, you can choose to take 1, 2, or all 3 slots. You must pay the registration fee for each attempt separately.
- **Normalization:** The questions are different in each slot, but the scores are normalized to ensure fairness. The highest score you achieve in any of the slots will be sent to the Symbiosis colleges you applied to.

---

## The 3-Attempt Master Plan: A Topper's Timeline

If you decide to register for multiple attempts, do not treat them as separate exams. Treat them as a single, coordinated project.

```
+------------------+     7-10 Days     +------------------+     7-10 Days     +------------------+
|    Attempt 1     |  ===============> |    Attempt 2     |  ===============> |    Attempt 3     |
| (Baseline/Mock)  |   Analyze & Fix   | (Target Score)   |   Optimize Speed  | (Peak Performance|
+------------------+                   +------------------+                   +------------------+
```

### Attempt 1: The Real-Life Baseline
- **Goal:** Get comfortable with the interface, the exam-hall environment, and the actual difficulty of the questions.
- **Focus:** Complete your strong sections first. Pay close attention to your time distribution and identify any questions that took too long.

### Attempt 2: The Core Target
- **Goal:** Correct the mistakes from Attempt 1.
- **Focus:** Adjust your attempt sequence based on the actual difficulty experienced in Slot 1. Focus on weak areas identified during mock analysis.
- **Stat:** Historically, the highest percentage of toppers secure their peak score in **Attempt 2**.

### Attempt 3: The Peak Effort
- **Goal:** Go for a high score.
- **Focus:** Because you already have two scores in the bank, you can afford to take calculated risks. Increase your speed and attempt count in your strong sections.

---

## Should You Register for All 3 Attempts?

The simple answer is **yes, if your budget permits**. 

According to past candidate statistics, **over 70% of students who took 3 attempts saw a score increase of 4 to 8 marks** between their first and best attempt. In SNAP's competitive structure, a 4-mark increase can be the difference between a 90 percentile and a 98.5 percentile (the cutoff for [SIBM Pune](/colleges/sibm-pune)).

### The Budget Consideration
If you cannot register for all three, register for **two attempts** (preferably Slot 1 and Slot 2). This gives you the safety net of a second attempt while keeping costs manageable.

---

## How to Prepare Between Attempts

The gap between SNAP slots is usually **7 to 10 days**. Here is how to spend that time:
- **Do not study new topics:** You cannot build new concepts in 7 days.
- **Analyze Slot 1 feedback:** MBA portals publish slot analyses immediately after the exam. Read these to understand which topics (e.g., specific arithmetic models or logical puzzles) were heavily tested.
- **Solve 2 short sectional tests daily:** Focus entirely on maintaining your calculation speed.

## ❓ Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I check my exam results online?
You can check results on the official website of the conducting bodies (e.g., NTA for JEE/NEET/CUET, or respective boards/testing organizations) using your application registration number.

### Are mock tests helpful in exam preparation?
Yes, attempting mock tests helps candidates build speed and accuracy, understand the exam pattern, and perform detailed analytics of strong and weak sections.

### What is the significance of negative marking in entrance exams?
Many competitive exams deduct marks for wrong answers (e.g., -1 or -0.25). Candidates should avoid guessing to maintain accuracy and prevent score drops.
